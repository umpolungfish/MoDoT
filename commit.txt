--mask: withhold an imscription without removing its address

A run must be able to refuse content it is supposed to derive — pre-existing
bip39_key_* entries, a private key already imscribed — without that refusal
deleting anything. The law is exact about which half moves: an enumerated address
is never removed, and its imscription is freely revisable. Withholding is the
weakest revision there is.

`--mask '<glob>'`, repeatable, `*` the only metacharacter. Applied once at catalog
load, so everything downstream inherits it — the scaffold, --entry injection, the
material and reaction lanes, the sweeps.

The address survives and stays counted: the catalog still reports 8444 entries
with five masked. What is withheld is everything associated with it — description,
tuple, tier, structural algebra — with `raw` reduced to the name alone. So the
lattice is intact and the content is gone, which is the distinction the law makes.

A masked address REFUSES rather than reads empty. Without that, click printed
Δ=? and carried on, which is a wrong answer wearing a shrug. It now says the
address exists and its content is withheld, because "not found" would send the
reader looking for a name that is right there.

And the masking is announced on every run, not only under --verbose. A run whose
catalog was altered has to say so, or a later reader cannot tell which content the
answer did and did not have.

Verified: masked entries refuse in click and in the monomer loader, unmasked
neighbours are untouched, and the entry count is unchanged.
