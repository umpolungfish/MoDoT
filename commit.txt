The condition was never the cost; the lookup was

vita and prooflift both crawl on long words while the host auditor answers the
same question in milliseconds, and the substrate was getting the blame.

`edges` is a flat Vec of pairs, and `successors(n)` scanned the whole edge list
and allocated a Vec on every call. The traversals call it once per node visited.
So `fsplit_ancestors` ran a full O(V·E) walk PER FORK, `between` ran two more PER
PAIR, and every one of those visits allocated. On a word of a few tokens nobody
notices; on a lifted proof term of tens of thousands it does not return.

Adjacency is now built once, both directions, and the two traversals read it.
Same verdicts, same condition, and the graph is untouched — only the lookup
changed.

Measured on the closure law's own proof, a word of tens of thousands of tokens,
through the bare-metal kernel: before, no return inside ten minutes. After,
verdict T in about eleven seconds wall-clock including boot. The host auditor
gives T on the same word in about eleven milliseconds, and that gap is now a
constant factor rather than a difference in kind.

One test in imasm16_3 fails and was failing before this change. Left alone,
noted, not adopted.
