--frames: which readings are the word's, and which are the cut's

We evaluate one frame of a ring and take what we saw there for a property of the
word. Every rotation is the same object and every frame is equally available to be
read, so a reported quantity is one of two things and the report has to say which:
INVARIANT, true of the word and readable from anywhere, or FRAME-BOUND, true of
the cut and meaningless quoted without it.

`--frames <word>` walks the whole orbit and partitions the readings. Invariant:
period, glyph multiset, the verdict where it holds across the orbit, and ring
transitions — which are counted WITH the closing edge, so rotation cannot move
them. Frame-bound: the final register with the cuts that reach each landing, the
opening glyph, and any absolute-position reading at all, since one rotation moves
every value into a different row.

It closes with which kind of word it is. Frame-free in its landing means every cut
rests in the same register and the evaluated frame carries no privilege.
PHASE-BEARING means the frames disagree, and then the one that was evaluated is
merely one of them and is not the word's own answer.

The case that motivated it: the migration and retirement ob3ects are DIFFERENT
words — the fuse sits in a different place — with identical landing maps at all
twelve cuts, A at 0,1,11 and Ftf at 2 through 5 and tf at 6 and T at 7 through 10.
Two constructions coming to rest the same way in every frame. Nothing read from one
cut could have told them apart or told them together.

Every ob3ect artifact records k=0 only — its word, tuple, verdict and topology are
all one frame — with nothing marking which of those survive rotation. This is the
instrument that says.
