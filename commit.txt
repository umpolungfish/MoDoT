--gradient: the eluent changes composition over the run

`--column` is isocratic — one eluent at one strength — and it cannot tell an
analyte the column never gripped from one that eluted first. Both sit at the
bottom of the same ranking. Gradient elution separates them, because it asks a
different question: not "how strongly is this held" but "at what mobile-phase
composition does the eluent's pull first exceed that hold".

`--gradient M1 M2 … on S from A to B [--steps N]` walks the eluent from a weak
component to a strong one, blending affinity linearly in the composition, and
records the step each analyte leaves. The composition IS the parameter, so a
gradient is a walk along it and retention is where that walk crosses the hold.

That makes four outcomes distinct where the isocratic tool had one ranking:

  eluted at step k   the walk crossed the hold, and k is the retention
  co-eluting         two analytes crossed on the same step — unresolved, and a
                     shallower gradient separates them if their holds differ
  RETAINED           the walk never reaches the hold; needs a STRONGER B, and a
                     longer run will not help
  VOID               the column never gripped it, so no eluent strength released
                     it — it separates from nothing and needs a different phase

The void case was a real defect on the first pass: with hold 0.00 the crossing
test fired at step 0 and reported four analytes eluting together, ranked as
though they had been separated. An analyte that never adsorbed has no retention
to report, so void is its own outcome rather than the earliest one.

Demonstrated: on stationary bip39_secp256k1_derivation, plutonium_normal reads
RETAINED with hold 1.67 against an eluent walking 1.67→1.42 — away from it — so
the tool says stronger B rather than more steps.
