The alphabet stops borrowing from arithmetic

    ⊢ VINIT   ⊣ TANCH   > AFWD    < AREV    ⋈ CLINK   ⊙ IMSCRIB
    ∈ FSPLIT  ∋ FFUSE   ⊤ EVALT   ⊥ EVALF   ⊞ ENGAGR  ◻ IFIX

Six marks move. CLINK was =, which is equality; EVALT and EVALF were + and ×,
which are sum and product; IFIX was ¬, which is negation and belongs to TNEG's
side of the language if to anything; and the dyad was ◇ ●, which meant nothing at
all and had to be taught. Every mark in the new set is a turnstile, a tack, a
membership, a join, or a box, and none of them arrives already saying something
this language does not mean.

The dyad is one opcode now, spelled ∈ ∋, with arity three. A fan of two is this
opcode with an empty arm rather than a different opcode, which is what the engine
already decided when the ancestry rule went arity-generic; the arity table just
had not caught up. FSPLIT3 and FFUSE3 keep their names as parse aliases and carry
the same glyph, since there is nothing left to distinguish.

The retired spellings ◇ ● = + × ¬ still parse. Every stored word loads, and what
the tools print is the new set: `imasm check ⊢◇>+●⊣` reports the same verdict as
`⊢∈>⊤∋⊣` and echoes the latter. Nothing in the catalog had to be rewritten.

The same twelve are the primitive alphabet, one glyph per axis in slot order, so
there is no mapping between two alphabets to maintain. There is one alphabet,
read as an operation or as an axis according to where it stands.
