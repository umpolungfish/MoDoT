One dyad, at whichever arity the carrier has

The guide has said for a while that ◇/● is the arity-2 case of ∈/∋, same
ancestry rule, same close condition, same register. The code did not do that. A
correctly wired tri dyad came back INVALID out of `imasm wire`, because check.rs
tested `== Token::Fsplit` literally everywhere, and `imasm protocol ⊢∈+×⊞∋⊣`
read the word as a bare line with no δ/μ dyad in it. The trilattice face lived in
a second machine that paired ∈/∋ with a split stack over the word read as a
cycle, nothing to do with ancestry over edges. Two engines for one grammatical
object, and the one that ran my words rejected half the alphabet.

What settles it is the register rather than the glyphs. δ cuts the four base
values into a PARTITION, and which partition depends on how many arms it has:
{T,t} | {F,f} at two, and {T} | {F} | {t,f} at three, the truth cut taken inside
the constructive block with the non-constructive pair held whole. Both are
partitions, so μ∘δ = id at either arity, which is the property the close
condition is about. And on the classical slice, where t and f are absent, the
three-arm cut IS the two-arm cut: the information arm carries nothing. ◇ is to ∈
what FOUR is to SIXTEEN_3. Not two operators, one operator and two carriers.

Worth being exact about what that does not say, because the loose version is
wrong in a way that matters: dropping an arm from ∈ does not give ◇. {T} | {F}
loses t and f and would not recover its input. They coincide on the slice, not by
truncation. The arity is not a choice either. ⊞ sets t and f together, so the
non-constructive pair can only ever be entered as a block, and three is what the
gate set leaves standing.

So the engine is arity-generic now. Fork and fuse are predicates over both
spellings; ancestry pairs a fuse with the fork ALL of its in-arms trace back to,
which reads as the old two-distinct-in-arms rule at two and as the tri-ancestral
rule at three, one threshold taken off the fuse's own in-degree; `protocol` wires
as many arms as the fork's arity declares, one along the chain and the rest
straight across; and the split gate takes the fan-out, so it fans the right
partition for the arity it actually has.

Measured, not argued. `⊢∈>+∋⊣` on the FOUR slice fans to T, F, N and the fuse
recovers B, flowing identically to `⊢◇>+●⊣`. The same word seeded A in SIXTEEN_3
fans to T, F, tf and recovers A. Both report id, lossless split-fuse. The
canonical words are unmoved: `⊢◇>+●⊣` T, `⊢◇⊙●⊣` N identity, `⊢◇⊞>●⊣` B paradox
held. `⊢∈⊙⊙⊙∋⊣` now reads N (identity) rather than N (no fork), which is the same
letter for the right reason.

One collision surfaced by putting both dyads in front of one checker, and it is
not fixed here: ⊞ is ENGAGR to the classic reading and EVALI to the trilattice
one. B beats T, so the classic checker answers B (paradox held) for the guide's
tri example `⊢>∈+×⊞≁∋¬⊣`, which the trilattice face calls T. One glyph, two
readings. That is the next thing the notation has to settle.

The glyphs themselves are untouched on purpose. Every stored word and all 49 type
programs are written in ◇ ●, and the spelling belongs to the notation pass, not
to this one.
